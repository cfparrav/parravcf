Two Months in Mexico

Hi,

This is the trip I had this year, starting May 26th and ending July 24th. It was the longest one I've had.

This is where I stayed:

  • Mexico City: May 26th - June 1st
  • Tepoztlán: June 1st - June 26th
  • Mexico City: June 26th - July 11th
  • Tijuana: July 13th - July 24th

I arrived in Mexico City with a main goal: to see System of a Down perform live on the 27th. Which went amazing. Luckily I wasn't alone the whole time. Shoutout to Petu 🗣️.

We really had a good time at the concert, and I got to see my favorite band for the first time. The following days were just me and Petu hanging out a couple of times, then I visited my dad, who was also there at that time. Good times.

My mind honestly was in another place for most of this part of the trip. I was a bit nervous about the future, and what came next was volunteering in Tepoztlán. Mind you, I'd never even heard of Tepoztlán a month before accepting the volunteer thing.

It was my first time volunteering, and my first time visiting this small town, and it surprised me the moment I got there. A beautiful, scenic town surrounded by hills, a lot of nature, and rain, of course.

The first day I got there, I met Vanessa, who became my best friend from this period of my life. We hung out whenever we could. She also had to deal with me being in an existential crisis for a few moments over there. Nothing serious, lol. I also had to deal with her a bit too 😅.

There were some moments here when I felt alone. It felt like I was out in the wilderness, with no phone signal, exposed to strange animals, and sleeping in a small spot... looking back, I don't regret any of it. But I did have a couple of rough days that first week.

This was the experience I was supposed to have in order to "find myself." Day by day, I felt better about where I was and started to look around and enjoy what I had.

I became good friends with the 3 dogs I had to take care of: Camila, Jose, and Renata. They were the best company for me. I really enjoyed feeding them day and night, even though I'm not really a dog person.

I was lucky to be in a town just an hour and a half from CDMX, so every chance I got (using my days off), I went to see the World Cup games there. I'd stay in hostels for 2 days every time Mexico played, then head back to Tepoztlán. I had a really good time, since Mexico won every game I caught in June.

Another highlight of the volunteer experience was helping build a pathway with Pancho, the gardener. We became good friends, and he taught me a lot of good old school music. He visited twice a week, and I always knew he was there when he turned on his radio. A good teacher with a lot of stories to tell.

The second week I was there, I met Serioshka, the owner of the place. Really nice person, very straightforward, with a good sense of art. Paola, another volunteer, also joined us that day.

There were a few days going out too. My favorite bar was "El Bicho," they had an amazing drink called "fungi negroni." I also remember Sara (Italian waitress who lived in Mexico), who I had really good conversations with. I really wish her the best wherever she is. I think she was supposed to be in Italy this past month or so. She told me she is a singer, but I couldn't see her perform since I left that day. :(

Another highlight for me was meeting Toni Kuhn, a very well-known cinematographer. What's interesting is I met him just by being on my own, taking a picture of the Tepozteco with a lot of fog surrounding it. He came up to me and told me he takes pictures too, and we started talking. A few weeks later, Vanessa and I ran into him again while out walking. Really interesting person.

My experience in Tepoztlán was really good in general. I miss being there right now, honestly. So relaxing, and I kept myself busy too.

I decided to end my volunteer program on June 26th, because of a new opportunity to meet up with one of my best friends in Mexico City. Saying goodbye came first, but I had a lot on my mind and couldn't fully process what I was doing. I even left so quickly that I forgot to do a few things.

Back in CDMX, I met up with my friend and we went out constantly. The first day we went to a bar called "Jardín Juárez," which ended up being my favorite because of the big screen they had for the games and the great vibe. I met a few people there too.

The best day for me was the Mexico game against Ecuador... unforgettable. The whole city looked like the world was about to end.

We had a good time with Daniel and Rachel, who we'd just met at the hostel. Rachel even filmed us talking about the World Cup and how we felt about the Mexican team.

Ngl, I had a few rough hungover days there, especially the day after I got to CDMX... I was hurting, fr. Not going to get into a lot of detail there lol.

After Daniel left, I moved to another hostel closer to downtown CDMX. My plan was to look for a few jobs in the meantime while I figured out when to head back to Hermosillo. Spoiler: I ended up just exploring CDMX instead, and I don't regret it.

I watched the Mexico vs. England game at my favorite bar by myself. Then I met up with Kevin, an old friend from my hometown, and we went bar hopping for a while. Good time, but I'm still sad about that result.

After that loss, I decided to head back to Hermosillo, but I missed my flight lol, so I stayed in a hotel inside a military base area close to the airport. I wasn't expecting it to be that hard to get in. Good security, and the hotel itself was super clean, everything was perfect. I heard President Claudia Sheinbaum was going to be there the next day, so that probably explained it.

It also caught me by surprise that there were 3 very important museums (all free entrance) right in front of the hotel. My next flight home wasn't until the following day, so I had all the time to visit them.

After the museums, I walked into a mall inside the military area, which even had a cinema. I bought a ticket just for myself to watch Toy Story 5 (cried a little bit) and grabbed something to eat after. One of my favorite days in CDMX.

The next day I arrived in Hermosillo very early, visited my grandma, and decided to drive to Tijuana to see my aunt the day after. I drove the whole way through the US route instead of going through Mexico. My grandma kept me very sharp and awake talking the entire drive until we reached San Diego, where we met up with my cousin.

Tijuana was hotter than I expected, turns out the heat isn't just a Hermosillo thing. My days there mostly consisted of trips to San Diego whenever we could, and eating and talking with my grandma and her daughter.

At this point the trip was basically in family mode, but I still went out a few times. I visited an old friend who lives there, Charlie, and we went to see a Xolos game too. Shame I couldn't stay for his birthday, I had to leave for Hermosillo that same day.

This whole stretch of the trip made me feel really good. I forgot about my normal routine entirely.

When I came back, I realized how hard it is to readapt to a normal schedule. Two months away helped me forget about all that, and opened my perspective to a lot of things.

I remembered I have free will, and that we can do whatever we want, whenever we actually have the chance to.
